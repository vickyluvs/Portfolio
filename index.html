About

I started out building websites — HTML, CSS, JavaScript and APIs and that's where I learned to debug patiently and read documentation until something makes sense. Wanting to understand what happens when things go wrong pulled me into cybersecurity, and I went back to school for a B.S. in Cybersecurity at the University of Maryland Global Campus.

Along the way I wrote a paper on the cyber risks facing small business owners, which turned into The Pink Proxy, my practice helping women-owned small businesses put basic security guardrails in place. Most of that work is the same work a good help desk does: account access, MFA, offboarding, and walking non-technical people through what changed and why.

I'm especially interested in identity and access management — who has access to what, and making sure that stays true over time.

Windows Event Log forensics: simulated attack investigation

I simulated attacker activity against a Windows Server from Kali Linux, then reconstructed what happened using Windows Event Viewer. Completed independently for CMIT 460 at UMGC, in an authorized, isolated lab.

  • Generated failed RDP logins with Hydra, then authenticated over SMB.
  • Exploited MS17-010 (EternalBlue) with Metasploit, created and deleted a user, and cleared the Security log.
  • Traced each step through the event logs and correlated timestamps, accounts and logon patterns.
  • Showed why centralized log forwarding to a SIEM preserves evidence an attacker tries to erase.
